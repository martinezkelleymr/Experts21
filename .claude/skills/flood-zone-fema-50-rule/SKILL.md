# Flood zone & FEMA 50% rule assessment

*Roster skill S07. Used by: `due-diligence-specialist` (owner), `permitting-code-specialist`, `insurance-risk-advisor`, `underwriting-analyst`, `renovation-project-manager`. Market facts are a Nov 2026 snapshot from `docs/`; refresh before relying on them.*

## Purpose
Stop deals that would force elevation or full flood compliance from being bought at a flip price.

## Rule summary
- Applies in Special Flood Hazard Areas (A, AE, VE zones). **X zones are not subject** to substantial-improvement requirements.
- If cumulative improvement cost ≥ **50% of the structure's market value (excluding land)**, the building must be brought into full compliance with current flood rules, usually **elevation** above base flood elevation (plus local freeboard), along with flood-resistant materials and vents.
- **Open permits count**: earlier permitted work is added to the new work's cost.
- Structure value is usually the Property Appraiser building value or an independent appraisal (with the local floodplain administrator's approval).

## Procedure
1. Get the flood zone (FEMA map, Boca Raton flood zone determination service, or county) and any elevation certificate.
2. Get the structure value (appraiser building value or appraisal) and compute 50% of it.
3. Total the planned scope cost at market labor rates (include contractor overhead and profit; owner labor counts at market value) plus open permits.
4. If ≥ 50%: rescope to stay under the threshold, price the elevation/rebuild, or pass on the deal. If 40–50%: flag it as high risk (change orders can push it over).
5. Note flood insurance cost (NFIP Risk Rating 2.0 / private) and lender requirements.

## Output
Zone, structure value, threshold, planned cost, % of threshold, verdict (safe / caution / triggers), options and costs.
