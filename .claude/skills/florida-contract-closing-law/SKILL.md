# Florida contract & closing law

*Roster skill S22. Used by: `real-estate-attorney` (owner), `transaction-coordinator`, `acquisitions-specialist`, `dispositions-specialist`. Market facts are a Nov 2026 snapshot from `docs/`; refresh before relying on them.*

## Purpose
Protect the company's position in every purchase and sale contract and avoid surprises at closing.

## Contract checklist (buy side)
- Form: FAR/BAR **AS IS** (an inspection period lets us walk away) or a custom investor contract.
- Buyer entity "and/or assigns" if assignment may be needed; check the assignment clause.
- Escrow deposit amount and escrow agent (title company); deposit release rules.
- **Inspection period** long enough for inspections, insurance quotes, flood/50% check and HOA review (10–15 days).
- Financing and appraisal contingencies; closing date; possession; occupancy (tenants?).
- Title: commitment review, objections period, survey, marketable title; municipal lien search (open permits, code liens).
- HOA/condo: estoppel, approval contingency, statutory rescission rights.
- Disclosures: Florida's known-defect disclosure duty (Johnson v. Davis), radon, lead paint (pre-1978), flood disclosure (Florida law requires flood history disclosures in residential sales; verify current requirements), HOA/condo disclosures.

## Closing costs (Palm Beach custom, negotiable)
Seller: deed doc stamps 0.70% and usually the owner's title policy. Buyer: lender's title policy, note doc stamps 0.35%, intangible 0.2%, recording, lender fees.

## Wholesale and double close
- Assignment: we assign our contract rights for a fee; disclose our role; don't market the property as if we owned it (Ch. 475 risk).
- Double close: A→B then B→C, with transactional funding or simultaneous closing; the title company must agree.

## Output
A clause-by-clause review with risks and redlines, a missing-terms list and closing cost estimates. **Final documents require a licensed Florida attorney.**
