# Florida landlord-tenant law (Ch. 83)

*Roster skill S24. Used by: `landlord-tenant-counsel` (owner), `property-manager`, `compliance-officer`. Market facts are a Nov 2026 snapshot from `docs/`; refresh before relying on them.*

## Purpose
Run rentals that are legally sound and evictions that succeed on the first filing.

## Key rules (verify the current statute)
- **Preemption (§83.425, 2023):** residential landlord-tenant regulation is preempted to the state; local tenant ordinances on notices, deposits and lease terms aren't enforceable. Fair housing laws still apply.
- **Security deposits (§83.49):** keep in a Florida bank account (with the required disclosure); give the deposit-holding notice within 30 days of receipt; after move-out, return within 15 days or send a certified **notice of intention to impose a claim** within 30 days.
- **Notices:** non-payment 3-day notice (excluding weekends and legal holidays); non-compliance 7-day notice (curable or non-curable); month-to-month termination needs **30–60 days'** notice.
- **Entry:** reasonable notice (24 hours) for repairs, at reasonable times.
- **Landlord duties (§83.51):** building, housing and health codes; extermination; locks and keys; garbage; heat; running water and hot water (single-family can shift some duties by written lease).
- **Evictions:** county court; uncontested often 3–6 weeks; the tenant must deposit rent into the court registry to defend a non-payment case. No self-help (lockouts or utility shutoffs are prohibited).
- **Squatters (HB 621, 2024):** sheriff removal by owner affidavit if the conditions are met.
- Required lease disclosures: radon; lead paint (pre-1978); landlord or agent name and address; deposit terms.

## Output
A step-by-step action plan with deadlines, notice templates outline (final versions by counsel), and a risks list. **Contested cases go to a licensed Florida attorney.**
