# Interior design, finish selection & staging

*Roster skill S16. Used by: `interior-design-staging-specialist` (owner), `renovation-project-manager`, `dispositions-specialist`, `brand-content-marketer`. Market facts are a Nov 2026 snapshot from `docs/`; refresh before relying on them.*

## Purpose
Spend renovation money where buyers and tenants notice it, and keep rentals durable.

## Finish tiers
| Tier | Use | Typical choices |
|---|---|---|
| Rental-durable | Buy-and-hold, BRRRR | LVP or porcelain tile throughout, quartz-look or entry quartz, shaker cabinets, satin nickel or black fixtures, semi-gloss neutral paint, no carpet |
| Flip mid ($600K–$1M) | Boca Square, Lake Ida, Central Boca | Large-format porcelain, white or 2-tone shaker, quartz, matte black/brass accents, frameless glass showers, LED, impact windows as a selling point |
| Flip upper ($1M+) | East Boca/Delray, historic | Custom or semi-custom millwork, natural stone, designer lighting, indoor-outdoor flow, pool and summer-kitchen upgrades |

## Principles
- Coastal-modern, light and bright palettes; match the neighborhood's comps, don't over-improve past the ARV ceiling.
- Prioritize kitchen, primary bath, flooring, curb appeal (paint, landscaping, front door, lighting), pool area.
- Highlight insurance-friendly features (new roof, impact glass) in listing materials.
- In historic districts, follow the approved COA design.
- Staging: furnish main rooms, outdoor living and the primary bedroom; then professional photos, drone and video.

## Output
A finish schedule (room, item, spec, cost tier), a mood/design direction summary, a staging plan and cost.
