# Historic preservation review

*Roster skill S13. Used by: `permitting-code-specialist` (owner), `renovation-project-manager`, `interior-design-staging-specialist`. Market facts are a Nov 2026 snapshot from `docs/`; refresh before relying on them.*

## Purpose
Know early whether a property is historic and plan design, timeline and cost around the review.

## Procedure
1. Check designation: city historic-district maps, the local register, individually designated sites, and contributing vs. non-contributing status.
2. Identify which work needs review: in Delray, **all exterior improvements and alterations** to designated properties or properties in districts need a **Certificate of Appropriateness (COA)**. Minor items (colors, materials, awnings) may get staff approval; major items (additions, demolition, elevations, window replacement style) go to the **Historic Preservation Board**.
3. Design to the standards (Secretary of the Interior's Standards; local guidelines): keep the massing, rooflines and window patterns; choose period-appropriate impact windows; demolishing contributing structures is very hard.
4. Add 1–3+ months for board review; budget for the architect and specialty materials.
5. Look for upside: premium ARVs in Del-Ida Park, Old Floresta and Marina; possible tax incentives (ad valorem exemptions for qualifying restorations).

## Output
Designation status, the review path (staff vs. board), design constraints, timeline and cost add, and a value/risk summary.
