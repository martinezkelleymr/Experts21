# 01 — Local Market Overview (Boca Raton & Delray Beach, FL)

*Research snapshot: November 2026. Figures come from public portals and broker reports that disagree with each other; always re-pull current MLS data before underwriting a deal.*

## Geography at a glance
- Both cities sit in **south Palm Beach County**, part of the Miami–Fort Lauderdale–West Palm Beach MSA, between Boynton Beach (north) and Deerfield Beach/Broward County (south).
- Key east–west corridors: Glades Rd, Palmetto Park Rd, Atlantic Ave (Delray's downtown spine), Linton Blvd. North–south: A1A, US-1/Federal Hwy, I-95, Military Trail, Florida's Turnpike, US-441/SR-7.
- Brightline has a Boca Raton station (downtown); Tri-Rail serves both cities. Transit access is a rent/resale amenity.
- "Boca Raton" and "Delray Beach" mailing addresses extend far west into **unincorporated Palm Beach County** (e.g., 33428, 33433, 33434, 33496, 33498 for Boca; 33445, 33446, 33484 for Delray). Unincorporated parcels are permitted by **Palm Beach County**, not the city — confirm jurisdiction on the Property Appraiser site (pbcpao.gov) for every deal.

## Prices (citywide, all property types unless noted)
| Metric | Boca Raton | Delray Beach |
|---|---|---|
| Zillow typical home value | ~$577K (Aug 2026, +3.7% YoY) | ZIP 33448 ~$342K (Jun 2026, −2.8% YoY) — city skewed by 55+ condos |
| Median sale price (various sources) | $552K–$665K; Redfin shows $820K–$855K (SFH-heavy mix) | $360K–$390K (all types); ~$700K (Redfin / SFH) |
| Time to contract | ~42–78 days | ~74 days (Houzeo) |
| Months of supply | Redfin Jan 2026: 8.0 (buyer's market) | Redfin Jan 2026: 8.6 (buyer's market) |

**Interpretation for agents:** The huge spread between "all types" and "single-family" medians is driven by the large stock of older condos/55+ communities (Kings Point, High Point, Century Village–style). Single-family detached homes east of I-95 commonly trade from ~$600K to several million; luxury Boca SFH median ≈ **$2.0M** (Jan 2026).

Palm Beach County overall (single-family, mid-2026): ~3.9 months supply, median ~$700K (+~12% YoY per one broker), median ~42 days to contract — i.e., **SFH is near balanced-to-tight, condos are a buyer's market.**

## Rents
- **Boca Raton houses:** ~$5,500–$5,700/mo average (Trulia, 2026); 3BR ≈ $4,800, 4BR ≈ $6,000. Rents roughly flat (+0.5% YoY).
- **Delray Beach:** apartments ~$2,450 (1BR) to ~$2,950 avg; single-family rent trend flat to slightly down (−2.6% YoY per one investor blog).
- MIAMI REALTORS (June 2026): 76% of Palm Beach County submarkets had higher asking rents YoY — rents bottomed after the 2024–25 softness.
- **Rent-to-price is thin.** At $600K–$800K purchase prices and $4.5K–$5.5K rents, gross yields are ~6–9% and many listings show **negative cash flow** after insurance, taxes, HOA and financing (BiggerPockets 33431/33432/33444 snapshots). Buy-and-hold only pencils with below-market acquisition, value-add, or larger down payments.

## Demand drivers
- **No state income tax**; continued in-migration of retirees, remote workers and finance/tech firms ("Wall Street South" narrative — partly promotional).
- Employers: Florida Atlantic University (~6,300), Boca Raton Regional Hospital (~3,100), Office Depot HQ, City of Boca Raton, NCCI, IBM, plus FAU Research Park (~1,500 jobs, avg salary ~$116K). D-Wave (quantum computing) relocating HQ to Boca Raton Innovation Campus (late 2026).
- Top-rated public schools (A-rated Palm Beach County district schools in Boca) support family rental demand.
- Delray Beach: Atlantic Ave dining/nightlife, beach, Pineapple Grove arts district; tourist and seasonal demand.
- **Seasonality:** "Season" (Nov–Apr) brings snowbirds — strongest months for listing flips and for furnished seasonal rentals.

## Investor activity
- Redfin Q1 2026: investors bought ~**20% of homes** in the West Palm Beach metro (Palm Beach County), ~1,245 purchases, +3% YoY, while investors pulled back elsewhere in Florida.
- BatchData Q1 2026: investors own ~14.7% of county SFH stock; **institutional (1,000+ homes) only ~3.8%** — the market is dominated by small/mid local investors.

## Major 2026 policy change: Amendment 3 (passed Nov 3, 2026, ~62%)
- Homestead non-school exemption rises to $150K (2027) and $250K (2028).
- **Non-homestead assessment-increase cap drops from 10% to 5%/yr starting Jan 1, 2027** — a direct benefit to rental holders (slower tax growth after purchase).
- Risk: local governments may raise millage rates to offset lost revenue; tax still resets to market value on each purchase.
- Expect owner-occupied demand to get a boost (lower carrying cost for homestead buyers) → supportive for **flip resale** values.
